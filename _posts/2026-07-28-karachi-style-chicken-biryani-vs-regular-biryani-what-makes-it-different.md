Ask anyone from Karachi and they'll tell you: biryani is not one dish, it's a hundred family arguments about the "right" way to make it. But a few things set true Karachi-style chicken biryani apart from the biryani you'll find elsewhere — and they're the same things we stick to every single Friday.



\## What Makes Karachi-Style Biryani Different?



Most biryanis are built around bold spice, but Karachi-style biryani is built around \*\*balance\*\* — a tangy, slightly smoky masala base, long-grain basmati rice that stays separate and fluffy (never mushy), and chicken that's marinated and slow-cooked until it falls off the bone.



\## The Secret Is in the Masala



The base masala combines whole spices — black cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, cumin — with ground red chili, coriander, and a handful of dried plums (aloo bukhara) that give Karachi biryani its signature tang. The chicken is cooked directly in this masala first, then layered with parboiled rice and finished on low heat (dum) so the flavors settle into every grain instead of just coating the surface.
